\begin{abstract}
Optimizing a learned reward model (RM) that is held fixed between updates produces a familiar symptom: proxy return rises while ground-truth return falls.
This symptom is routinely attributed to ``reward hacking'' or ``overoptimization,'' but it conflates two mechanistically distinct failures:
(i)~\emph{RM accuracy decay}, where the policy leaves the preference-support region and the RM becomes unreliable out of distribution; and
(ii)~\emph{overoptimization}, where the RM remains confident yet systematically wrong and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) exploits that flaw (Goodhart).
We formalize both modes, introduce causal induction protocols that assign ground-truth labels by construction, and evaluate a multi-signal diagnostic---ensemble disagreement, preference-support novelty, accuracy probes, and policy geometry---as a classifier.
Across three continuous-control variants and five seeds, the diagnostic separates the modes well above chance.
Mode-conditional mitigations behave asymmetrically: expanding preference coverage helps decay but not planted misspecification, while KL regularization and uncertainty penalties exhibit the complementary pattern under shared blind spots.
Our results argue that conflating the two modes leads to the wrong fix, and that disagreement alone is insufficient---consistent with ensemble herding under shared errors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Preference-based RL and RLHF optimize a proxy RM $r_\theta$ in place of an inaccessible gold reward $r^\star$~\citep{lee2021pebble,gao2023scaling}.
When $r_\theta$ is frozen---between PEBBLE-style updates~\citep{lee2021pebble}, or entirely under a fixed proxy---policy improvement can drive $\hat R\uparrow$ while $R^\star\downarrow$.
Prior work measures this divergence~\citep{gao2023scaling} or mitigates it with ensembles and uncertainty~\citep{eisenstein2024helping,coste2023reward,advpo2024,urm2024,inform2024}, typically treating ``overoptimization'' as a single phenomenon.

We argue this is a category error.
\textbf{Decay} is a failure of the RM under distribution shift; \textbf{overoptimization} is exploitation of a stable misspecification.
They share a training curve but demand opposite interventions.
A naive rule ``high ensemble disagreement $\Rightarrow$ decay; agreement $\Rightarrow$ overoptimization'' fails when ensemble members share blind spots~\citep{eisenstein2024helping}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Formal definitions of Decay vs.\ Overopt under frozen proxies, with identifying assumptions.
  \item Causal induction protocols that label runs by construction (restricted support + noise vs.\ planted misspecification).
  \item A multi-signal diagnostic evaluated with leave-one-seed-out AUROC, plus feature ablations.
  \item Mode-conditional mitigation experiments and a shared-blind-spot negative control.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Overoptimization scaling.}
\cite{gao2023scaling} characterize gold vs.\ proxy vs.\ KL curves; they do not identify OOD decay vs.\ in-support exploitation.
\textbf{Ensembles.}
\cite{eisenstein2024helping,coste2023reward} show ensembles mitigate but do not eliminate hacking when errors are shared.
\textbf{Mitigations.}
Uncertainty penalties, robust policy optimization, and information-bottleneck RMs~\citep{advpo2024,urm2024,inform2024} improve robustness without causal mode labels.
\textbf{PbRL.}
PEBBLE/B-Pref~\citep{lee2021pebble,lee2021bpref} provide intermittent freezes ideal for frozen-window measurement.
\textbf{Taxonomies.}
Goodhart variants~\citep{manheim2018categorizing} and specification gaming vs.\ goal misgeneralization~\citep{krakovna2020specification,langosco2022goal} motivate our operational split for frozen learned RMs.

\section{Problem Formalization}
Let $\{r_{\theta_k}\}_{k=1}^{K}$ be an RM ensemble, $\bar r_\theta$ its mean, and $u(s,a)=\mathrm{Var}_k[r_{\theta_k}(s,a)]$.
A \emph{frozen window} holds $\theta$ fixed while $\pi$ updates.
A window exhibits the \emph{symptom} if early-to-late $\Delta\hat R>0$ and $\Delta R^\star<0$.

\paragraph{Decay.}
Proxy error grows because $d_\pi$ leaves preference support $\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{D})$: novelty $N(d_\pi;\mathcal{D})$ and disagreement $\mathbb{E}_{d_\pi}[u]$ rise; on-policy oracle preference accuracy falls.

\paragraph{Overoptimization.}
$d_\pi$ remains largely in-support (or on a region where $r_\theta$ is calibrated to its training objective), but $\arg\max \bar r_\theta \neq \arg\max r^\star$ on an exploit manifold $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hack}}$.
Disagreement and novelty stay low while gold collapses---especially under shared ensemble initialization.

Identification requires constructing protocols where these clauses hold by design (details in Appendix / code docs).

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Environment and proxy}
We use a 2D point-mass suite (three geometry/reward variants) with gold reward $r^\star$ (goal progress $-$ control cost) and misspecified $r_{\mathrm{wrong}}$ (drop control cost; add velocity bonus), inducing a high-actuation exploit manifold.
SAC~\citep{haarnoja2018sac} optimizes a frozen proxy after a preference phase.

\subsection{Induction protocols}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Oracle:} preferences from $r^\star$; proxy $=$ ensemble mean.
  \item \textbf{Decay:} restricted support + label noise; proxy $=$ ensemble trained on sparse/noisy prefs.
  \item \textbf{Overopt:} preferences from $r_{\mathrm{wrong}}$; SAC optimizes analytical $r_{\mathrm{wrong}}$ (planted Goodhart) while the ensemble is retained for diagnostics.
  \item \textbf{Shared blind spot:} as Overopt with identical ensemble initialization (herding control).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Diagnostic features}
For late frozen-window snapshots we record:
ensemble disagreement, $k$NN preference-support novelty, held-out / on-policy / fixed-ref preference accuracy, action entropy, and $\mathrm{KL}(\pi\|\pi_{\mathrm{init}})$.
A logistic classifier is trained leave-one-seed-out; hack-manifold mass is used only for induction validation, not as a classifier input.

\subsection{Mitigations}
Uncertainty penalty on proxy rewards; KL-to-init regularization; expanded preference coverage (``more prefs'').
We predict asymmetric effects by mode.

\section{Experiments}
\paragraph{Setup.}
Five seeds; three env variants; $25$k frozen-proxy steps after preference fitting; ensemble size $K{=}3$.
Primary metric: AUROC for Decay (positive) vs.\ Overopt/Shared-blindspot (negative) on baseline (no mitigation) runs.
Secondary: mode-conditional true-return under mitigations.

\section{Discussion}
Wrong mitigations fail: uncertainty penalties are weak under shared blind spots; KL alone does not repair a thin preference support; more on-policy preferences do not remove a planted misspecification.
LLM transfer is a natural extension (synthetic gold RM \`a la~\cite{gao2023scaling}) but is not required to establish the continuous-control identification result.

\section{Conclusion}
Proxy--gold divergence under frozen RMs is not one failure mode.
By inducing Decay and Overopt by construction and evaluating multi-signal diagnostics as classifiers, we show the modes are separable and that the correct intervention depends on which mode is present.
