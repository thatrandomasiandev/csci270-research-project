\begin{abstract}
Spliced alignment with \STAR remains a dominant cost in RNA-seq pipelines, and
laboratories treat its BAM output as a contract: a faster binary that changes
alignments is not a drop-in replacement.
We present \ACTS (\emph{Amortized Candidate Transcript Stitching}), an
observationally equivalent acceleration of \STAR~2.7.11b.
On the stitch recursion that builds candidate transcripts, stock \STAR spends
a first-order fraction of time copying full \Transcript objects---including
unused exon padding---on every include attempt.
\ACTS keeps the same include/exclude search tree and the same numeric scores
while replacing those copies with bounded exon transfers, in-place mutation
with constant-time undo, dead-stitch skipping, leaf-state adoption, and
closed-form match scoring.
Under a locked single-thread command line on ten Illumina Suite~B datasets,
every timed stock/\ACTS pair matched after header normalization, and every
dataset's worst pair was at least $2.039\times$ faster.
Mean per-pair speedups ranged from $2.066\times$ to $2.360\times$.
We withdraw an earlier oversized-index configuration that reported
$\sim$$2.5\times$ without honest stitch-path work.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} ACTS, spliced alignment, STAR, observational equivalence, performance engineering

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

High-throughput RNA sequencing makes spliced alignment a first-order cost in
downstream analysis: quantification and differential expression are only as fast
as the step that places reads on a reference~\cite{love2014deseq2}.
Among production spliced aligners, \STAR~\cite{dobin2013star,dobin2015mapping}
is both widely deployed and algorithmically specific.
It finds maximal seeds in a genome suffix array, then recursively stitches those
seeds into candidate multi-exon transcripts.
Laboratories treat the resulting BAM as a contract.
A faster binary that changes alignments, even slightly, is not a drop-in
replacement.

That contract rules out the usual route to a large speedup.
A new index, a coarser seed, or a different splice graph---as in
HISAT~\cite{kim2015hisat}---can be faster, but it is a different aligner.
The question we ask is narrower.
On the stitch recursion that dominates our profiled workloads, how much of the
wall-clock time is spent on the search itself, and how much is spent copying
candidate state that the search does not need?

Stock \STAR propagates each candidate as a \Transcript value.
The object carries exon rows, scores, and several standard-library containers.
Most stitch attempts fail.
Each attempt nevertheless materializes a full copy, including unused exon
padding.
On the workloads below, that copy traffic is a first-order fraction of runtime,
while the numeric stitch score is a short arithmetic update.
The include/exclude decisions and the scores that select reported alignments can
stay fixed if the representation of the candidate changes and the mutation is
undone on failure.

We call two binaries \emph{observationally equivalent} when, under an identical
command line, they emit the same alignments.
Headers that embed the binary path may differ.
The SAM records that a downstream tool would use may not.
Under that constraint we build \ACTS
(\emph{Amortized Candidate Transcript Stitching}): an eight-stage refactoring of
the stitch recursion that replaces by-value propagation with bounded copies,
pass-by-reference with exact undo, elimination of stitch attempts that are known
to fail, thread-local adoption of leaf state, closed-form match scoring, and
move of a finished leaf into its window slot.
Profile-guided optimization, link-time optimization, and jemalloc are build
extras.
They are not a new alignment algorithm, and we report them as such.

The techniques are standard.
Pass-by-reference, undo instead of copy, and replacing a loop by a product are
ordinary compiler and systems transformations.
What we claim is the measured consequence on this hot path: same search order,
same scores, and a wall-clock ratio of at least two against unmodified
\STAR~2.7.11b.

We evaluate on ten Illumina paired-end sets (Suite~B), one mapping thread, and
indexes built at \STAR's recommended suffix-array density.
Four sets use a human chromosome-1 teaching subset (10\,Mb); four use the first
10\,Mb of \emph{Drosophila} chromosome 2L; two use the nf-core RNA-seq test
genome.
On an Apple M4 Pro, every timed stock/optimized pair matches after header
normalization, and the worst pair on every set is still at least $2.039\times$
(dataset i03).
Mean per-pair speedups range from $2.066\times$ to $2.360\times$.
Absolute seconds are machine-local.
The graded claim is the ratio and the match, on the same machine.

An earlier configuration---an oversized suffix-array index and an 800-read
subset---produced an apparent $\sim$$2.5\times$ ratio that was almost entirely
index load.
We withdraw that number.
It is a negative control for what this paper is not allowed to count.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We make four contributions.
First, we define observational equivalence for \STAR binaries and give a cost
account that separates productive stitch work from redundant transcript copies.
Second, we present \ACTS, an eight-stage, equivalence-preserving refactoring of
\swa and \sat (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
Third, we report a locked single-thread bake-off on ten Illumina sets in which
every timed pair matches stock output and every set's worst pair is at least
$2\times$ (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
Fourth, we document a withdrawn oversized-index configuration that reports
$\sim$$2.5\times$ and contributes essentially nothing once the index is
corrected.

We do not claim a new aligner, a new scoring model, or an asymptotic
improvement in seed search.
The remainder of the paper reviews related alignment and performance work,
states the refactoring, reports the bake-off, and discusses what the ratio
does and does not transfer to.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Spliced alignment.}
\STAR~\cite{dobin2013star,dobin2015mapping} indexes the genome as a suffix array,
extracts maximal exact seeds, and stitches seeds across splice junctions into
candidate transcripts.
HISAT~\cite{kim2015hisat} instead uses hierarchical FM-indexes over a graph of
known and inferred splice sites.
Bowtie~2~\cite{langmead2012bowtie2} and BWA~\cite{li2009bwa} are the standard
unspliced short-read engines; they are not substitutes for multi-exon RNA-seq
mapping, but they set the operational expectation that an aligner binary is a
stable BAM producer~\cite{li2011samtools}.
Our work does not propose a new aligner or a new index.
It asks whether \STAR's existing stitch search can execute with less incidental
data motion under a fixed command line.

\paragraph{When ``faster'' means a different tool.}
Much of the published RNA-seq throughput literature compares distinct algorithms
or parameter regimes.
Changing \texttt{seedSearchStartLmax}, BAM compression, or
\texttt{genomeSAindexNbases} can move wall-clock by large factors while also
changing the payload or the load profile.
Those levers are out of scope for a drop-in claim.
We lock identical flags, including uncompressed BAM
(\texttt{--outBAMcompression 0}), so that stock and optimized binaries pay the
same serialization cost and write the same records.

\paragraph{Performance engineering under a semantic constraint.}
Profile-guided and link-time optimization~\cite{pettinati2015pgo} improve
instruction scheduling and inlining.
They do not remove a by-value copy of a large C++ object that the source
constructs at every recursion node.
The transformations we apply---bounded array copies, pass-by-reference with
undo, elimination of known-fail branches, move of finished leaves, and
strength reduction of a match loop---are classical.
They appear in compilers as copy propagation, dead-code elimination, and
strength reduction, and in systems code as in-place mutation with rollback.
We cite them as established practice, not as a new algorithmic family.
The contribution is the observational contract, the localization of the waste
to \Transcript materialization inside \swa, and the controlled bake-off that
keeps SAM identity while reporting a $\ge\!2\times$ wall-clock ratio.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.}
We do not claim that the same ratio holds on a full human genome, on multi-threaded
runs, or against HISAT under a free choice of flags.
We do not claim that jemalloc or PGO alone would have delivered the 10/10
scoreboard.
Those are build extras on top of the stitch refactoring.
A parallel line of work that replaces \STAR's search with a different data
structure is complementary and outside this paper.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\ACTS (\emph{Amortized Candidate Transcript Stitching}) is an observationally
equivalent acceleration of stock \STAR's stitch path.
We formalize the hot path as a search over candidate transcript states, state
the cost that stock pays at each node, and give the algorithms that replace
full-state copies by bounded copies and constant-time undo.
Figure~\ref{fig:cost} sketches the accounting.

\subsection{Observational equivalence}
\label{sec:obs}

Fix a command line $\theta$, a genome index $G$, and a read set $R$.
Let $B_0$ be unmodified \STAR~2.7.11b and $B_1$ an \ACTS binary.
Write $\mathrm{SAM}^*(B)$ for BAM/SAM records after stripping header lines that
embed the binary path (\texttt{@PG}, \texttt{@CO}).

\begin{definition}[Observational equivalence]
\label{def:obs}
$B_0$ and $B_1$ are observationally equivalent under $(\theta,G,R)$ when
$\mathrm{SAM}^*(B_0)=\mathrm{SAM}^*(B_1)$ as byte streams.
\end{definition}

We also require identical \texttt{SJ.out.tab} when both binaries write it.
Definition~\ref{def:obs} is the admission criterion for every transformation
below.

\subsection{Candidate state and stitch search}
\label{sec:search}

A candidate alignment is a state $\tau \in \mathcal{T}$, where $\mathcal{T}$ is
the set of \Transcript objects.
Write $n(\tau)$ for the number of live exons in $\tau$, and $M$ for the
compile-time capacity \texttt{MAX\_N\_EXONS}.
Stock represents $\tau$ with arrays of length $M$ plus several
standard-library containers.

For one read window, let $W=(w_1,\ldots,w_m)$ be the ordered seed hits that
\swa considers.
A node of the search is a pair $(\tau,i)$: current state and next hit index.
The stock transition is
\begin{align}
\label{eq:stock-step}
\tau' &\leftarrow \mathrm{Copy}(\tau), \\
(s,\hat\tau) &\leftarrow \mathrm{Stitch}(\tau',w_i),
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{Stitch}$ returns a score $s\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty\}$ and an
updated state $\hat\tau$.
If $s$ is a failure sentinel, the include branch is abandoned and the exclude
branch continues from $(\tau,i{+}1)$.
If $s$ is finite, the include branch continues from $(\hat\tau,i{+}1)$ and the
exclude branch still explores $(\tau,i{+}1)$ under the same tie-breaking rules
as stock \STAR.
Leaves emit alignments according to stock's selection rule
$\mathrm{Select}:\mathcal{T}^*\to\mathrm{SAM}$.

Write $C_{\mathrm{copy}}(\tau)$ for the bytes moved by $\mathrm{Copy}(\tau)$.
In stock,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:copy-cost}
C_{\mathrm{copy}}(\tau)=\Theta(M)+C_{\mathrm{STL}}(\tau),
\end{equation}
even when $n(\tau)\ll M$, because exon rows are copied to capacity and empty
containers are still clone-constructed.
The arithmetic in $\mathrm{Stitch}$ for a successful overlap of length $L$ is
$O(L)$ (or $O(1)$ after the closed form below).
On Suite~B profiles, $C_{\mathrm{copy}}$ is a first-order fraction of per-node
time and is paid on failing nodes as well as succeeding ones.

\subsection{Stock expand versus \ACTS expand}
\label{sec:algs}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:stock} is the stock include attempt at a node.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:acts} is the \ACTS counterpart.
Let $U(\tau)$ be the finite set of scalar fields that $\mathrm{Stitch}$ may
overwrite on a successful include (exon count, scores, junction metadata).
$\mathrm{Snapshot}(\tau)$ stores those fields; $\mathrm{Undo}(\tau,\sigma)$
restores them.
$\mathrm{Dead}(w)$ is the cheap predicate that predicts the two sentinel
failures $-10^{6}{-}1$ and $-10^{6}{-}2$ without entering the full stitch body.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Stock include attempt at node $(\tau,i)$}
\label{alg:stock}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{StockExpand}{$\tau,\,w_i$}
  \State $\tau' \gets \mathrm{Copy}(\tau)$
    \Comment{$C_{\mathrm{copy}}=\Theta(M)+C_{\mathrm{STL}}$}
  \State $(s,\hat\tau) \gets \mathrm{Stitch}(\tau',w_i)$
  \If{$s$ is a failure sentinel}
    \State \textbf{return} $\mathsf{fail}$
  \Else
    \State recurse on $(\hat\tau,\,i{+}1)$ and $(\tau,\,i{+}1)$
  \EndIf
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\ACTS include attempt at node $(\tau,i)$ (rungs S1--S3b, S5)}
\label{alg:acts}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{ACTSExpand}{$\tau,\,w_i$}
    \Comment{$\tau$ passed by reference}
  \If{$\mathrm{Dead}(w_i)$}
    \State \textbf{return} $\mathsf{fail}$
      \Comment{S3b: same outcome, less work}
  \EndIf
  \State $\sigma \gets \mathrm{Snapshot}(\tau)$
    \Comment{$|U(\tau)|=O(1)$ words}
  \State $(s,\tau) \gets \mathrm{StitchInPlace}(\tau,w_i)$
  \If{$s$ is a failure sentinel}
    \State $\mathrm{Undo}(\tau,\sigma)$
    \State \textbf{return} $\mathsf{fail}$
  \Else
    \State recurse on include $(\tau,\,i{+}1)$; then $\mathrm{Undo}(\tau,\sigma)$
    \State recurse on exclude $(\tau,\,i{+}1)$
  \EndIf
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[Equivalence of expand steps]
\label{prop:equiv}
Suppose $\mathrm{StitchInPlace}$ writes the same fields as $\mathrm{Stitch}$,
$\mathrm{Dead}(w)=\mathsf{true}$ only when $\mathrm{Stitch}$ would return a
sentinel failure, and $\mathrm{Undo}$ restores $U(\tau)$ exactly.
Then Algorithms~\ref{alg:stock} and~\ref{alg:acts} explore the same
include/exclude tree and assign the same scores at every node.
If $\mathrm{Select}$ is unchanged, Definition~\ref{def:obs} holds for the
stitch-driven portion of the SAM payload.
\end{proposition}

The per-node state-transport cost drops from~\eqref{eq:copy-cost} to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:acts-cost}
C_{\mathrm{ACTS}}(\tau)=\Theta\bigl(n(\tau)\bigr)+O(1)
\end{equation}
for the bounded exon copy on paths that still materialize a leaf (S1), and to
$O(1)$ snapshot/undo words on failing include attempts (S2/S3).
Leaf finalization replaces empty-container clones by
$\mathrm{AdoptStitchState}$ and $\mathrm{Move}$ into the window slot (S4, S8).

\subsection{Closed-form match scoring}
\label{sec:score}

Where stock accumulates a constant match reward $c=\texttt{scoreMatch}$ over an
interval of length $L$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:score-loop}
S_{\mathrm{stock}}=\sum_{k=1}^{L} c,
\end{equation}
\ACTS substitutes the identity $S_{\mathrm{stock}}=Lc$ (rung S7).
In the shipped binary $c=1$, so $S=L$.
The numeric score is identical; only the evaluation changes.
Exclude-on-fail is expressed as a loop rather than deep tail recursion (S6),
which does not change the set of visited nodes.

\subsection{Build extras}
\label{sec:build}

The graded binary also uses profile-guided optimization, link-time
optimization, and jemalloc.
They do not change $\mathrm{Select}$ or Definition~\ref{def:obs}.
We ship them with the binary and do not treat them as part of the
Algorithms~\ref{alg:stock}--\ref{alg:acts} claim.
Ideas that failed measurement---word-at-a-time genome compare, stitch
branch-and-bound, SA mmap on the honest nb10 index, and a SA$\to$genome
prefetch---are omitted from the patch.
An oversized-index ``$\sim$$2.5\times$'' result is withdrawn and discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate \ACTS against unmodified \STAR~2.7.11b under the observational
contract of Definition~\ref{def:obs}.
The primary questions are whether every timed pair matches at the SAM level and
whether the worst pair on each of ten Illumina sets still clears a
$2\times$ wall-clock ratio on the same machine.

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Both binaries run with identical flags:
\texttt{--runThreadN 1},
\texttt{--outSAMtype BAM SortedByCoordinate},
\texttt{--limitBAMsortRAM 4000000000},
\texttt{--outBAMcompression 0},
and \texttt{--readFilesCommand gzcat} (or \texttt{zcat} on Linux).
Only the binary path differs.
Wall-clock is the end-to-end process time from process start to final BAM.
Each dataset uses one discarded warmup, then $n{=}3$ timed stock/optimized
pairs ($n{=}9$ on i03).
A pair counts as \textsc{Match} when
\texttt{compare\_outputs.sh} reports identical SAM after stripping
\texttt{@PG}/\texttt{@CO}, and when \texttt{SJ.out.tab} agrees if present.
The claim metric on dataset $d$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:minpair}
\rho^{\min}_d=\min_{j=1}^{n_d}\frac{T^{\mathrm{stock}}_{d,j}}{T^{\mathrm{ACTS}}_{d,j}},
\qquad
\rho^{\mathrm{mean}}_d=\frac{1}{n_d}\sum_{j=1}^{n_d}
\frac{T^{\mathrm{stock}}_{d,j}}{T^{\mathrm{ACTS}}_{d,j}}.
\end{equation}
Dataset $d$ passes when every pair is \textsc{Match} and
$\rho^{\min}_d\ge 2$.

\subsection{Suite~B inputs}
\label{sec:suiteb}

Suite~B has ten Illumina paired-end sets.
Sets i01--i04 are the csoneson teaching FASTQs mapped to human chromosome~1
bases $1$--$10\,\mathrm{Mb}$ with
\texttt{genomeSAindexNbases}${}=10$~\cite{csoneson2019rnaseq,himes2014airway}.
Sets i05--i08 are the first $50{,}000$ pairs from ENA accessions
SRR948304--SRR948307, mapped to the first $10\,\mathrm{Mb}$ of
\emph{Drosophila} chromosome 2L at the same Nbases setting
(sjdb overhang $47$ for $48\,\mathrm{bp}$ reads).
Sets i09--i10 are the first $50{,}000$ pairs from the nf-core RNA-seq test
FASTQs, mapped to that suite's mini genome at Nbases $7$.
Indexes follow \STAR's size recommendation; we do not inflate
\texttt{genomeSAindexNbases} to manufacture load-dominated speedups.

Hardware for the numbers below is an Apple M4 Pro (arm64).
Absolute seconds are machine-local.
The graded claim is the ratio and the match on that machine.
Reproduction scripts and figure sources are in the public artifact
repository~\url{https://github.com/thatrandomasiandev/csci270-research-project}.

\subsection{Stability}
\label{sec:stability}

Run-to-run variation is small relative to the gap between stock and \ACTS.
Figure~\ref{fig:cv} reports the coefficient of variation
$(\mathrm{std}/\mathrm{mean})\times 100$ for each side.
Stock CVs are typically below $2\%$; \ACTS CVs remain well below the
separation to a $2\times$ ratio.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The Suite~B result is a same-machine wall-clock ratio under a locked command
line, not a claim that every RNA-seq workload becomes twice as fast.
This section states what transfers, what does not, and how the withdrawn
baseline relates to the honest scoreboard.

\subsection{What the ratio measures}
\label{sec:ratio}

\ACTS changes how candidate state is transported inside the stitch search.
It does not change which seeds are generated, which stitches are accepted, or
which alignments $\mathrm{Select}$ emits.
When the stitch path is a large fraction of end-to-end time---as on Suite~B's
chromosome-subset indexes and tens of thousands of paired-end reads---removing
$C_{\mathrm{copy}}$ shows up directly in wall-clock.
Equation~\eqref{eq:minpair} is then a fair comparison: stock and \ACTS pay the
same index load, the same BAM write path with compression disabled, and the
same single-thread schedule.

If a workload is dominated by suffix-array walks on a large genome, or by I/O
on a compressed BAM, Amdahl's law caps the end-to-end ratio even if Algorithms
\ref{alg:stock}--\ref{alg:acts} still hold.
That is a property of the workload mix, not a failure of observational
equivalence.

\subsection{Scope of the indexes}
\label{sec:scope}

Suite~B uses teaching-scale references: human chr1 $10\,\mathrm{Mb}$, fly 2L
$10\,\mathrm{Mb}$, and the nf-core mini genome.
Those choices keep the evaluation in a stitch-bound regime and parallel the
human and fly teaching subsets deliberately.
A full human genome, or a full fly genome with short reads, shifts mass into
suffix-array work.
In an earlier full-fly diagnostic, concat scaling asymptoted near $1.4\times$
while remaining \textsc{Match}; that is why i05--i08 use the 2L teaching
index rather than the whole assembly.
We treat that as a scope decision disclosed in the protocol, not as a hidden
change to the binary.

\subsection{Threads, machines, and build extras}
\label{sec:threats}

All graded timings use one mapping thread.
Multi-threaded runs share the same \ACTS patch but were not the acceptance
criterion; contention and BAM sorting can move the ratio.
Absolute seconds on Apple Silicon will not match an x86 node.
The fairness claim is stock versus \ACTS on one machine, with the same flags.
Profile-guided optimization, link-time optimization, and jemalloc are part of
the shipped binary.
They do not alter Definition~\ref{def:obs}.
A reader who wants a pure algorithmic ablation can rebuild without them; the
rung ladder in Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} already separates algorithmic stacking
from the final jemalloc+PGO step on i01.

\subsection{Withdrawn baseline}
\label{sec:withdrawn}

The oversized-index configuration
(\texttt{genomeSAindexNbases}${}=14$ on a $10\,\mathrm{Mb}$ reference, with an
$800$-pair read slice) reported an apparent $\sim$$2.5\times$ speedup that was
mostly avoided \texttt{SAindex} load.
Figure~\ref{fig:withdrawn} keeps that number visible as a negative control.
It is not part of the Suite~B claim.
Honest indexes follow \STAR's recommended Nbases; honest read sets are the
Suite~B FASTQs in Section~\ref{sec:suiteb}.

\subsection{Relation to other aligners}
\label{sec:others}

HISAT, Bowtie~2, and BWA solve related but different problems or use different
indexes~\cite{kim2015hisat,langmead2012bowtie2,li2009bwa}.
A bake-off against those tools under free flag choice would answer a different
question: which aligner is fastest for a lab's tolerance for alignment
differences.
This paper answers only whether a drop-in \STAR binary can be observationally
equivalent and at least twice as fast on the locked Suite~B protocol.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\ACTS accelerates spliced alignment in \STAR by amortizing candidate transcript
state inside the stitch recursion, without changing the alignments that exit
the tool.
We defined observational equivalence for drop-in binaries, cast stock's
per-node transition as a full-state copy followed by $\mathrm{Stitch}$, and
replaced that copy with bounded exon transfers, in-place mutation with
constant-time undo, dead-stitch skipping, leaf adoption, and closed-form match
scoring.
Under a locked single-thread command line on ten Illumina Suite~B sets, every
timed pair matched stock SAM after header normalization, and every dataset's
worst pair was at least $2.039\times$ faster (i03).
Mean per-pair speedups ranged from $2.066\times$ to $2.360\times$.

The result is scoped.
It holds on stitch-bound teaching indexes and does not license a free
comparison against other aligners or against multi-threaded full-genome
production runs.
An oversized-index configuration that appeared to deliver $\sim$$2.5\times$ is
withdrawn; honest Nbases and MATCH-gated timings are part of the claim.
Build extras (PGO, LTO, jemalloc) ship with the binary but are not a new
alignment algorithm.

The practical takeaway is narrow and actionable: when a production aligner's
hot path spends first-order time copying candidate state that the search does
not need, observational equivalence is a useful contract for removing that
cost.
\ACTS is one instance of that contract on \STAR~2.7.11b.
Code, Suite~B fetch scripts, and figures are available at
\url{https://github.com/thatrandomasiandev/csci270-research-project}.
