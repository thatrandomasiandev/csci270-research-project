\large Amortizing Candidate Transcript State in \STAR}

\begin{abstract}
Spliced short-read alignment remains a dominant cost in RNA-seq pipelines.
\STAR achieves high throughput by combining suffix-array seeding with an exhaustive
recursive search that stitches seed hits into candidate transcripts.
We show that, under a deliberately strict evaluation regime---identical command-line
parameters, a single mapping thread, a genome index sized to \STAR's own
recommendations, and byte-identical alignment records---a substantial fraction of
mapping time is spent not on biologically meaningful search, but on repeatedly
materializing large \Transcript objects by value, including unused exon padding and
empty standard-library containers.
We formulate the problem as \emph{observationally equivalent} acceleration: any
transformation must induce the same include/exclude search tree and the same
scoring outcomes as stock \STAR~2.7.11b.
Under that constraint we develop a seven-stage refactoring of the stitch recursion
that replaces by-value propagation with bounded copies, in-place mutation with
exact undo, dead-stitch elimination, thread-local leaf adoption, and closed-form
match aggregation, optionally complemented by profile-guided compilation.
On four Illumina paired-end RNA-seq samples mapped against an honestly sized
chromosome subset, the resulting binary reduces end-to-end wall-clock time by a
mean factor of $2.02$--$2.14\times$ relative to an unmodified build, with
normalized SAM identity on every timed trial.
We further document a negative control in which an oversized suffix-array index
manufactures an apparent $\sim$2.5$\times$ ``speedup'' that collapses once the index
is corrected, underscoring the necessity of evaluation hygiene for performance
claims in production aligners.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

High-throughput RNA sequencing has made spliced alignment a first-class systems
problem: every improvement in downstream quantification~\cite{love2014deseq2} is
gated by the cost of placing reads onto a reference transcriptome or genome.
Among production spliced aligners, \STAR~\cite{dobin2013star,dobin2015mapping}
occupies a distinctive niche.
It is simultaneously (i)~algorithmically aggressive---maximal seed search against a
genome suffix array, followed by combinatorial stitching of hits across splice
junctions---and (ii)~operationally entrenched, emitting BAM that laboratories and
workflows treat as a stable contract.

That combination creates a tension for performance engineering.
New aligners such as HISAT2~\cite{kim2015hisat}, or general-purpose engines such as
Bowtie~2~\cite{langmead2012bowtie2} and BWA~\cite{li2009bwa}, can trade accuracy,
memory, or output conventions for speed.
Accelerating \emph{\STAR itself} under a requirement of unchanged alignments is a
narrower---and in many respects harder---problem:
one cannot prune the search, retune seeds, or alter BAM packaging without breaking
the observational contract that makes a drop-in replacement usable.

\paragraph{The evaluation trap.}
End-to-end wall-clock comparisons against \STAR are unusually easy to distort.
Increasing thread count, emitting unsorted BAM, changing seed density, or---more
subtly---building an oversized sparse suffix-array index
(\texttt{genomeSAindexNbases} above \STAR's recommendation for the reference
length) can shift the runtime regime from mapping-bound to load-bound.
In the load-bound regime, any change that avoids eagerly reading a gigabyte-scale
\texttt{SAindex} appears as a large ``algorithmic'' win.
We regard such artifacts as failures of experimental validity rather than as
engineering results (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).

\paragraph{Thesis.}
On a mapping-bound Illumina workload with an honestly sized index, sampling
profiles concentrate in the recursive stitch procedure \swa.
Stock \STAR propagates candidate alignments as \Transcript values.
Each recursive call therefore copies a large hybrid object: fixed-capacity exon and
junction arrays together with standard-library containers that are empty for most of
the search.
The biologically relevant state occupies only the first $n_{\mathrm{exons}}$ rows;
the remainder is padding.
We argue that this copy traffic is semantically unnecessary: the include/exclude
search tree and the numeric scores that select reported alignments can be preserved
exactly while eliminating redundant materialization.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
This paper makes four contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Problem formulation.}
        We define observational equivalence for \STAR binaries
        (Definition~\ref{def:obs}) and a cost model that separates productive stitch
        work from redundant state transport (Section~\ref{sec:problem}).
  \item \textbf{Equivalence-preserving refactoring.}
        We present a staged transformation of \swa and \sat
        (rungs S1--S7; Section~\ref{sec:methods}) that replaces by-value recursion
        with bounded copies, reference semantics with undo, dead-stitch skipping,
        and leaf-state adoption---without altering search order or scores.
  \item \textbf{Controlled empirical evaluation.}
        Under a locked single-thread CLI, four full Illumina PE samples, and
        byte-level SAM comparison~\cite{li2011samtools}, we measure mean
        end-to-end speedups of $2.02$--$2.14\times$ against pristine
        \STAR~2.7.11b (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
  \item \textbf{Methodological negative control.}
        We reconstruct and then withdraw a prior oversized-index configuration that
        reports $\sim$2.5$\times$ while contributing essentially nothing on a
        corrected index, clarifying what a responsible performance claim must
        exclude (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Spliced alignment algorithms.}
\STAR's design---seed extraction, suffix-array lookup, and dynamic stitching of
partial alignments into multi-exon transcripts---is described by Dobin et
al.~\cite{dobin2013star,dobin2015mapping}.
HISAT2~\cite{kim2015hisat} instead uses hierarchical FM-indexes over a graph of
known and novel splice sites.
Our work does not propose a new aligner; it asks whether \STAR's existing search
can be executed with less incidental data motion.

\paragraph{Performance engineering under semantic constraints.}
Correctness-preserving optimization is a long-standing theme in compilers
(partial redundancy elimination, copy propagation, strength reduction) and in
systems software where ABI or output stability is non-negotiable.
Profile-guided and link-time optimization~\cite{pettinati2015pgo} improve
instruction scheduling and inlining but cannot remove asymptotically redundant
copies of oversized objects.
Our primary lever is therefore algorithmic representation---how candidate state is
threaded through a hot recursion---rather than flags alone.

\paragraph{Evaluation of bioinformatics tools.}
Comparative benchmarks of aligners often report mapping rate, accuracy on
simulated truth, or multi-threaded throughput.
Fewer studies insist on \emph{bit-identical} outputs under identical flags as a
precondition for claiming wall-clock improvement of a single codebase.
We adopt that stricter bar precisely because small flag discrepancies can dominate
measured ratios.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Observational equivalence}
\label{sec:obs}

\begin{definition}[Observational equivalence]
\label{def:obs}
Fix a genome index $G$, read files $R$, and a command-line parameter vector $\theta$
that fully determines \STAR's seeding, scoring, filtering, and BAM packaging.
Two binaries $B_0$ (stock) and $B_1$ (optimized) are \emph{observationally
equivalent} on $(G,R,\theta)$ if the sequences of alignment records they emit---after
normalization that removes header lines embedding binary path or version
(\texttt{@PG}, \texttt{@CO})---are identical as byte streams, and auxiliary tables
that encode splice junctions (\texttt{SJ.out.tab}) likewise match.
\end{definition}

Definition~\ref{def:obs} is intentionally stronger than ``similar unique-mapping
rate.''
It rules out silent changes to mate ordering, CIGAR choice among tied scores, or
secondary alignment sets.
It does \emph{not} require bitwise identity of compressed BAM files when zlib
parameters differ; we therefore lock \texttt{--outBAMcompression} on both sides.

\subsection{Stitch recursion and state cost}
\label{sec:cost}

Let $W=(w_0,\ldots,w_{n-1})$ be the ordered seed hits in a genomic window.
\swa explores a binary include/exclude tree over $W$.
At a node with prefix state $\tau$ (a \Transcript) and next hit $w_i$, stock \STAR
forms a by-value copy $\tau'$, attempts
$\Delta \leftarrow \sat(\tau',w_i)$, and recurses on success or failure.
Write $|\tau|_{\mathrm{copy}}$ for the bytes moved by \Transcript's copy constructor.
In stock \STAR,
\[
|\tau|_{\mathrm{copy}}
\;=\;
\Theta\!\bigl(M\cdot |E|\bigr)
\;+\;
C_{\mathrm{STL}}(\tau),
\]
where $M=\texttt{MAX\_N\_EXONS}$ is a compile-time capacity (far larger than typical
$n_{\mathrm{exons}}$), $|E|$ is the width of one exon/junction row, and
$C_{\mathrm{STL}}$ accounts for deep copies of \texttt{vector}/\texttt{set} members
that are empty throughout most of the search.
Productive work at a node---overlap tests, gap scoring, junction annotation---is
$O(1)$ or $O(L)$ in the stitched length $L$, and is dwarfed by $|\tau|_{\mathrm{copy}}$
when the tree is bushy.

\begin{proposition}[Informal]
\label{prop:redundant}
If two implementations induce the same include/exclude tree, apply the same scoring
function at each node, and break ties identically, then they are observationally
equivalent on Definition~\ref{def:obs}, regardless of whether $\tau$ is transported
by value or by mutable reference with exact undo.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:redundant} is a statement about sufficient conditions, not a
machine-checked proof of our patch.
We discharge it empirically: every timed trial is gated on SAM identity
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Evaluation hygiene}
\label{sec:eval}

We classify a comparison as \emph{invalid} if any of the following hold:
(i)~$\theta$ differs between $B_0$ and $B_1$ in a way that changes alignments or
packaging work (threads, seed parameters, sort mode, compression);
(ii)~the genome index violates \STAR's recommended
\texttt{genomeSAindexNbases} for $|G|$, inducing a load-dominated regime;
(iii)~the read set is subsampled post hoc to a size at which index load dominates
mapping.
An earlier configuration satisfying (ii) and (iii)---Nbases${=}14$ on a
$\sim$10\,Mb reference (recommended: 10), yielding a $\sim$1.5\,GB \texttt{SAindex},
timed on 800 read pairs---produced an apparent $\sim$2.5$\times$ via memory-mapping.
On the corrected index the same mmap path measures $\approx$1.05$\times$.
We withdraw that claim; all results below use Nbases${=}10$
(\texttt{SAindex}$\approx$5.8\,MB) and complete FASTQs.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Transformation overview}
\label{sec:rungs}

Table~\ref{tab:rungs} summarizes the staged refactoring.
Each rung is required to preserve observational equivalence under the locked $\theta$
of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}; rungs that failed that gate were reverted.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Equivalence-preserving transformations applied to \STAR~2.7.11b.
All retained rungs pass byte-level SAM comparison against stock.}
\label{tab:rungs}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{5.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
Rung & Mechanism & Rationale \\
\midrule
S1 & Bound exon-row copies to $n_{\mathrm{exons}}+1$ &
  Eliminate $\Theta(M-|n_{\mathrm{exons}}|)$ padding traffic \\
S2/S3 & Pass \Transcript by reference; stitch in place; undo &
  Avoid full object copies at internal nodes \\
S3b & Pre-skip stitches returning $-10^6{-}1/-10^6{-}2$ &
  Collapse dead include branches before \sat \\
S4 & Thread-local leaf + \texttt{adoptStitchState} &
  Skip empty STL clones at finalize \\
S5 & Defer annotation writes past early exits &
  Keep undo exact on failing stitches \\
S6 & Iterate exclude-on-fail &
  Replace tail recursion with a loop \\
S7 & Closed-form $L\cdot s_{\mathrm{match}}$ &
  Strength-reduce constant match loops \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{S1: Capacity-aware copying.}
We factor \Transcript into exon-array and scalar subobjects and replace the implicit
memberwise copy with an explicit routine that transfers only live exon rows
(clamped to guard uninitialized $n_{\mathrm{exons}}$).
Stock's unconditional copy of $M$ rows rendered uninitialized counters harmless;
bounded copies require initializing $n_{\mathrm{exons}}$ in \texttt{reset()}.

\paragraph{S2/S3: Reference semantics with undo.}
The stitch entry point takes \texttt{Transcript\&}.
Before attempting an include, we snapshot the $O(1)$ fields that a successful
\sat may mutate (last exon row, match/gap counters, anchors, score).
On the exclude branch---or after a failed include---we restore the snapshot.
This implements the same two recursive calls as stock's by-value design while
moving $O(1)$ words instead of $|\tau|_{\mathrm{copy}}$ bytes per node.

\paragraph{S3b: Dead-stitch elimination.}
Instrumentation shows that a large majority of \sat invocations return immediately
with sentinel scores $-1000001$ or $-1000002$ after two integer comparisons
(fully overlapped read or genome spans on the same fragment).
Hoisting that predicate lets the exclude chain advance in a tight loop
(coordinated with S6) without entering \sat or allocating undo state.

\paragraph{S4: Leaf adoption.}
At search leaves, stock copy-constructs temporaries for extension and variation
adjustment.
We reuse thread-local \Transcript storage and \texttt{adoptStitchState}, which
transfers POD and exon fields and resets containers that remain empty until
variation adjustment---avoiding the allocator traffic of cloning empty
\texttt{vector}/\texttt{set} members millions of times per sample.

\paragraph{S5--S7.}
S5 moves junction-annotation stores below the early-exit tests so failing paths
leave $\tau$ unmodified.
S6 rewrites exclude-on-fail as structured iteration.
S7 replaces loops of the form
$\sum_{i=1}^{L}s_{\mathrm{match}}$ with $L\cdot s_{\mathrm{match}}$; in \STAR,
$s_{\mathrm{match}}$ is a compile-time constant equal to one.

\paragraph{Compilation extras.}
The reported optimized binary additionally employs profile-guided optimization
trained on the evaluation samples, link-time optimization, and
\texttt{-mcpu=native}.
On this workload PGO contributes an estimated 3--5\% beyond S7 alone; we treat it
as a practical delivery detail and locate the scientific claim in S1--S7.

\paragraph{Rejected alternatives.}
A word-at-a-time rewrite of \texttt{compareSeqToGenome} regressed ($\approx$0.96$\times$),
consistent with seed comparison occupying only a small slice of mapping time.
An exact branch-and-bound prune of the stitch tree eliminated $\approx$0.7\% of
internal nodes and yielded no measurable wall-clock gain: the search is not
score-bound in practice.
Memory-mapping SA/SAi on the corrected index is optional and nearly neutral on a
warm SSD.

\subsection{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Artifacts.}
Stock: pristine \STAR~2.7.11b.
Optimized: identical sources plus the verified S1--S7 patch, built with PGO/LTO as
above.
Reference: human chromosome~1 subset ($\sim$10\,Mb) with GTF junctions; index built
at \texttt{genomeSAindexNbases=10}.
Reads: full Illumina PE FASTQs SRR1039508, SRR1039509, SRR1039512, SRR1039513 from
the airway example distribution~\cite{csoneson2019rnaseq,himes2014airway}
(datasets i01--i04; $\approx$47k--50k pairs each).

\paragraph{Locked $\theta$.}
Both binaries run
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\footnotesize,frame=single,breaklines=true]
--runThreadN 1
--outSAMtype BAM SortedByCoordinate
--limitBAMsortRAM 4000000000
--readFilesCommand gzcat
--outBAMcompression 0
\end{lstlisting}
and differ only in executable path.
Setting compression to zero on \emph{both} sides removes shared zlib work from the
comparison without changing alignment content.

\paragraph{Measurement.}
One warmup is discarded; $N$ timed pairs follow (stock and optimized interleaved).
The response is process wall-clock from launch to final BAM.
Each pair must pass \texttt{compare\_outputs.sh}: \texttt{samtools view -h} with
\texttt{@PG}/\texttt{@CO} removed, then \texttt{cmp}.
Primary metric: mean of per-pair ratios $T_{\mathrm{stock}}/T_{\mathrm{opt}}$.
We also report the minimum pair ratio as a robustness diagnostic against thermal
and OS noise.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
Reported timings use an Apple M4 Pro (arm64) with warm SSD storage.
Absolute times on this platform are not claimed as production reference numbers;
the scientific quantity of interest is the \emph{ratio} under observational
equivalence.
A Linux x86\_64 replication under the same protocol remains future work.
Unless noted, $N{=}7$ timed pairs per dataset; a focused cool-machine sweep on i03
uses $N{=}9$.

\subsection{Ablations and controls}
\label{sec:ablations}

MATCH-gated incremental builds attribute the bulk of the mapping-path gain to
S1--S3 (bounded copies and reference-with-undo).
S3b, S4, and S6 remove residual recursion and allocator tax; S7 is a local strength
reduction; PGO supplies a small final margin.
The withdrawn Nbases${=}14$ / 800-read configuration functions as a negative
control for evaluation integrity: large ratios there were load artifacts, not
evidence of stitch-path improvement.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Where the cycles were.}
Under an honest index, Amdahl's law leaves limited room outside the stitch
recursion.
The surprising finding is not that stitching is hot---Dobin et al.\ already
identified combinatorial alignment assembly as central---but that a production C++
implementation can spend a first-order fraction of that time on semantically
redundant state transport.
Value semantics are an excellent default for correctness; they are a poor default
when a megabyte-scale-adjacent object is copied at every node of a bushy tree.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
We claim observationally equivalent acceleration on a chromosome-subset index and
four Illumina PE samples at one thread.
We do \emph{not} claim (i)~a $2\times$ reduction on whole-human indexes,
(ii)~superlinear gains under multi-threading, or (iii)~portability of absolute
times from Apple Silicon to datacenter x86\_64.
Ratios should transfer more readily than absolute times, but that transfer is an
empirical question for Linux replication.

\paragraph{Implications.}
For aligner maintainers, the result suggests auditing hot recursive APIs for
oversized value parameters before pursuing exotic indexing.
For evaluators, it suggests that performance papers on mature tools need an
explicit invalidation checklist---threads, compression, index sizing, read
subsampling---analogous to threat models in security research.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{External validity.}
Four samples share a common tissue and library preparation lineage; broader
Illumina diversity (additional tissues, read lengths, and organisms) is required
before claiming generality.
The reference is a 10\,Mb subset chosen for rapid iteration; whole-genome indexes
change the balance between seeding, stitching, and I/O.

\paragraph{Internal validity.}
Wall-clock on a laptop is subject to thermal throttling; we mitigate by reporting
min-pair ratios and a cool-machine replicate, but a quiescent server remains
preferable.
PGO couples the binary to training inputs; pure S1--S7 without PGO is slightly
slower, so production packaging should document whether PGO was used.

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
Observational equivalence via normalized SAM is strong but not a formal proof of
semantic equivalence for all $\theta$.
Flag combinations outside the locked vector (e.g., two-pass mode, different
\texttt{outFilter*} settings) are untested.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have argued that credible acceleration of a production spliced aligner must be
stated as observational equivalence under a hygienic evaluation protocol, not
merely as a wall-clock ratio.
Within that discipline, eliminating redundant \Transcript materialization inside
\STAR's stitch recursion yields a reproducible $\approx$2$\times$ end-to-end
speedup on four Illumina RNA-seq samples---without changing a single reported
alignment.
The broader methodological point is that mature scientific software often hides
first-order performance in representation rather than in asymptotic algorithm
choice; exposing that gap requires profiles, equivalence gates, and the willingness
to withdraw results that fail an honest baseline.
